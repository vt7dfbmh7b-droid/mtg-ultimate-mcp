# MTG Ultimate V0.4 — Commander Rules + Rules-Aware Simulation

V0.4 separates **hard Magic/Commander rules** from **simulation assumptions**, then makes the default Monte Carlo engine materially more rules-aware.

## Hard Commander deck-construction rules

The `check_commander_rules` tool validates resolved decks before advanced simulation or optimization.

### Color identity

Every card in the deck must have a color identity that is a subset of the combined color identity of the commander(s).

Examples:

- A black-red (`BR`) commander may use black, red, black-red, and colorless cards.
- It may not use a white, blue, or green identity card.
- A hybrid `{B/R}` card has both black and red in its color identity under the current Commander rule.
- With two legal commanders, their color identities are combined.
- Lands with basic land types are checked so those intrinsic mana colors do not fall outside commander identity.

The engine uses Scryfall's Oracle-level `color_identity` field for rules identity while preserving set code + collector number + finish as the separate physical-printing identity used for pricing.

### Commander eligibility and two-commander configurations

V0.4 recognizes:

- one ordinary legendary-creature commander
- cards whose Oracle text explicitly says they can be your commander
- Partner
- Friends forever
- Choose a Background + legendary Background
- Doctor's companion + Doctor
- matching Partner with pairs

Invalid two-commander pairings are rejected rather than silently combining their colors.

### Deck size, legality, and singleton

The engine checks:

- exactly 100 cards including commander(s)
- 99 library cards with one commander or 98 with two
- each designated commander appears once
- every resolved card is Commander-legal
- Commander banned/not-legal status
- singleton by English card name
- unlimited basic lands
- Oracle copy-count exceptions such as “a deck can have any number…”
- bounded copy-count exceptions such as “a deck can have up to nine…”

If cards cannot be resolved, the result is `incomplete`; the engine does not falsely declare the deck legal.

## V0.4 rules tools

### `check_commander_rules`

Full hard rules validation for a pasted Commander deck.

### `check_card_for_commander`

Answers questions such as:

- Can this blue card go in my Rakdos deck?
- Is this hybrid card legal with my commander?
- Is this land legal in this color identity?
- Are these two commanders a legal pair?

The normal `analyze_deck`, `simulate_deck_consistency`, and `suggest_upgrades` paths now also use the same hard rules layer. Fully resolved illegal lists are not treated as valid simulation/optimization inputs.

## V0.4 mana-base intelligence

### `analyze_mana_base_v04`

Uses resolved Oracle text and land types to analyze:

- produced colors
- commander-identity mana sources
- shock lands
- check lands
- fast lands
- slow lands
- reveal lands
- multiplayer/bond lands
- unconditional tapped lands
- other conditional tapped lands without inventing a condition
- restricted mana text
- detected cost reducers
- fetch/search lands
- exact fetch targets that actually exist in the submitted deck

A fetch target must satisfy the source card's search clause and be present as a resolved land in the deck. For example, a “Swamp or Mountain card” fetch does not gain imaginary access to an Island merely because the simulator wants a color.

## Default V0.4 Monte Carlo sequencer

`simulate_deck_consistency` now routes through the V0.4 rules-aware turn sequencer.

### Land sequencing happens inside each simulation

The simulator evaluates common land patterns against the simulated board/hand state:

- shock lands — chooses the untapped tempo line and records life paid
- check lands — checks relevant basic land types already controlled
- fast lands — checks the current number of other lands
- slow lands — checks the current number of other lands
- reveal lands — checks whether an appropriate land can be revealed from hand
- multiplayer/bond lands — uses the configured opponent count
- always-tapped lands
- other unknown conditional patterns — conservatively treated as tapped rather than guessed

Fetches mutate the actual simulated library. A fetch only finds a legal target still present in that library, removes the target, puts it onto the battlefield, and applies the target/search tapped rules.

### Restricted mana and cost reducers

Common `Spend this mana only to...` restrictions are enforced during payment for:

- commander casting
- creature spells
- artifact spells
- instant/sorcery spells

Commander-identity sources use the resolved combined commander identity rather than generic five-color access.

Common generic cost reducers are deployed and applied to matching spell/commander costs, including commander tax where applicable.

### Tutors now find actual legal targets

Common tutors are parsed for:

- any-card searches
- creature/artifact/enchantment/instant/sorcery/planeswalker/land restrictions
- basic-land restrictions
- basic land types
- simple mana-value ceilings
- hand/top/battlefield/graveyard destinations

When a requested combo is being measured, a tutor must be cast and actually be capable of finding a missing combo piece. The selected card is removed from the simulated library and moved to the parsed destination. The old “any tutor approximately equals any missing card” shortcut is no longer the default simulation behavior.

### Draw effects create actual card flow

One-shot draw effects pull extra cards from the remaining simulated library after they resolve.

Simple recurring engines are also modeled for common patterns such as:

- upkeep draw
- end-step draw
- attack-trigger draw proxy
- combat-damage draw proxy

Those engines add extra future card flow after deployment, so simulations can distinguish a deck that merely contains draw cards from one that successfully casts its engines early enough to matter.

## Pod pressure and interaction windows

### `simulate_pod_pressure_v04`

This first requires a fully resolved, Commander-legal deck, then layers explicit opponent-pressure assumptions over the V0.4 consistency engine:

- commander-removal pressure
- configurable key-spell interaction pressure
- configurable board-reset pressure
- first-cast affordability
- +2 and +4 commander-tax recast affordability
- commander uptime/recast proxies
- combo attempt pressure
- protection-density proxy for challenged attempts

Built-in profiles:

- `goldfish`
- `casual`
- `core`
- `upgraded`
- `optimized`
- `cedh`

Users may override:

- `commanderRemovalPerTurn`
- `keySpellInteractionChance`
- `boardResetPerTurn`

The numerical interaction rates are returned in every result. They are **scenario assumptions**, not universal observed frequencies and not claimed win rates.

## Rule fact vs simulation assumption

MTG Ultimate keeps these separate.

**Rules facts**

- color identity
- deck size
- singleton restrictions
- card/commander format legality
- commander eligibility
- valid two-commander pairing mechanics
- actual Oracle text
- actual set / collector printing identity
- whether a parsed tutor/fetch restriction matches a candidate card
- common parsed mana spending restrictions

**Simulation assumptions**

- how frequently opponents remove a commander
- how frequently a key spell is challenged
- how often a board reset occurs
- whether simple attack/combat-damage draw engines successfully trigger once per future turn
- strategic choices among multiple otherwise-legal tutor/fetch targets

This prevents an estimated gameplay behavior from being presented as if it were a Magic rule.

## Current limitations / next simulation depth

V0.4 is substantially more rules-aware but is still not a complete four-player Magic rules engine.

Next depth should focus on:

1. exact alternate casting costs, Phyrexian mana, delve, convoke, improvise, affinity, and conditional free spells
2. treasure/token mana generated by conditional game actions
3. deeper zone-aware combo requirements rather than generic “pieces accessible” assembly
4. board-state-aware opponent removal target choice
5. specific counterspell/protection stack exchanges and priority passes
6. commander presence feeding into every commander-dependent permanent/trigger
7. combat/blocker state rather than the current transparent recurring attack-draw proxy
8. calibration against observed tournament/reference samples without converting correlation into fake causation
