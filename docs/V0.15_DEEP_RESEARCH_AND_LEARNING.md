# V0.15 Deep Research and Learning

V0.15 is an experimental layer on top of the current Commander/cEDH engine. It is deliberately not promoted to the stable runtime yet.

The goal is not to add a vague "AI score". The goal is to make MTG Ultimate better at separating strong evidence from repeated noise, learning from validated outcomes, and refusing to learn around hard Magic rules or physical-printing restrictions.

## Deep research

V0.15 treats different source classes as evidence for different questions.

- Official sources are strongest for rules and product facts.
- Scryfall remains the structured source for Oracle identity, legality and exact physical printings.
- Commander Spellbook remains the curated source for known combo structures.
- TopDeck.gg provides structured tournament outcome evidence when configured.
- EDHTop16 remains an attributed public competitive reference until a current stable structured API is independently verified.
- EDHREC, Archidekt, Moxfield and similar sources are useful for adoption, deck construction and primers, but popularity is not treated as proof of performance.
- Playgroup.gg can provide a different class of observed paper-game evidence.
- DeckCheck is an independent analysis opinion, not a ground-truth power oracle.
- TCGfind NZ and market sources answer availability/price questions rather than strategic optimality.

The research synthesizer scores observations using source weight, freshness, sample size, access mode and outcome strength. It also has an explicit `independentGroup` concept so two websites that ultimately repeat the same tournament, decklist or dataset are not counted as two independent confirmations.

Contradictory evidence is retained. Strong support and strong opposition can produce a `disputed` verdict rather than being averaged into false certainty.

Freshness is question-specific. Pricing and local availability decay quickly, tournament/metagame evidence decays faster than stable card identity, and official rules evidence decays slowly.

## Adaptive learning baseline

The first active learning baseline is intentionally a transparent logistic ranker.

It can learn weights for signals such as:

- simulation improvement,
- tournament support,
- cross-class research support,
- independently verified combo evidence,
- mana efficiency,
- interaction efficiency,
- price efficiency,
- community support.

A deterministic holdout split is required before learned weights are considered promotable. Small datasets or weak holdout accuracy block promotion.

The transparent model is the baseline a more complex learner must beat. Complexity by itself is not evidence of better MTG decisions.

## Experimental neural model

V0.15 now also contains an actual neural candidate rather than describing logistic regression as "deep learning".

`neural-ranker-v15.ts` implements a deterministic two-hidden-layer multilayer perceptron with:

- the same evidence/strategy feature family as the transparent model,
- two configurable hidden layers,
- tanh hidden activations,
- sigmoid output,
- seeded initialization,
- gradient-based backpropagation,
- L2 regularization,
- binary log-loss evaluation,
- a direct holdout comparison against the transparent logistic baseline.

The neural model is experimental and shadow-only. Its unit tests include a nonlinear interaction that a linear baseline cannot represent, deterministic same-seed training, and hard-gate rejection before neural inference.

A neural model is only considered a useful shadow candidate when it has enough unseen holdout examples, reaches an acceptable holdout accuracy, and materially beats the transparent baseline on the same unseen data. That still does **not** make it production-ready for MTG decisions.

## Provenance-safe learning corpus

`learning-corpus-v15.ts` adds the data layer needed before real strategic learning can be trusted.

Each labelled outcome can carry:

- outcome identity,
- observation time,
- source identity,
- evidence class,
- independent evidence group,
- leakage group,
- exact deck fingerprint,
- commander identity,
- model features,
- outcome label,
- optional importance and metadata.

The exact deck fingerprint includes commander/main zone, quantity, card name, set, collector number and finish. Reordering a decklist does not change the fingerprint, while changing the physical printing does.

Corpus deduplication is conservative: an identical deck used in another event is not automatically considered a duplicate. Repeated representations of the same observed outcome inside the same independent evidence group can be collapsed.

Temporal splitting is leakage-group aware. If one event/series/deck-history group crosses the temporal holdout boundary, the whole leakage group is kept on one side rather than allowing closely related examples to leak into both training and evaluation.

The corpus audit exposes duplicate rate, positive/negative balance, temporal coverage, independent evidence-group count, evidence-class count and leakage-group count. These values feed the deep-learning readiness decision rather than allowing model training to hide poor data quality.

## Non-learnable hard gates

No learned model can override:

- Commander legality,
- exact 100-card validation,
- unresolved card/printing failures,
- exact physical-printing restrictions.

These are rule/data constraints, not preferences to be learned away.

## Deep-learning readiness gate

V0.15 includes a separate readiness evaluator before any neural model is treated as justified for real MTG learning.

The default promotion bar requires, at minimum:

- enough labelled examples to make model complexity worthwhile,
- healthy positive/negative label balance,
- meaningful temporal coverage,
- multiple independent evidence groups,
- multiple evidence classes,
- low duplicate/dependent-data rate,
- passed leakage checks,
- a sizeable temporal holdout set,
- acceptable candidate accuracy,
- a material improvement over the transparent baseline on unseen temporal data.

The current defaults prefer at least 2,000 labelled examples for promotion, at least 200 temporal holdout examples, at least three independent evidence groups and three evidence classes, no more than a 10% duplicate rate, and at least a two-percentage-point gain over the transparent baseline.

A neural candidate that cannot beat the transparent model on unseen temporal data should not be promoted merely because it is more complex.

## cEDH control evidence

The unrestricted Kinnan control now proves the construction pipeline can do more than find a theoretical combo. With artificial theme/price restrictions removed, the live end-to-end test builds a legal 100-card deck, independently re-resolves every exact card identifier, reproduces deterministic Commander Spellbook wins, and passes the engine's strong competitive construction signals for curve, early density, fast mana and free interaction.

The combo-seed ranker was also corrected after a live test exposed a flaw: pure compactness/popularity initially preferred a legal but clunky Leveler/Jace line. Seed selection now adds practical execution cost, dead-card risk, utility outside the combo and commander overlap before choosing a construction seed.

This is still construction evidence, not a claim that the generated list is globally optimal for a live cEDH metagame. Tournament/decklist research and future real outcome learning are intended to close that gap.

## Promotion strategy

V0.15 should remain experimental until deterministic CI, server-construction tests, live research smoke tests and the learning-data pipeline are clean.

The neural model should first run in shadow mode: make rankings without controlling production choices, compare its recommendations against the transparent model and real outcomes, then promote only if it wins reliably on leakage-safe temporal holdouts without increasing rules/printing failures.

Stable runtime remains unchanged until that evidence exists.
