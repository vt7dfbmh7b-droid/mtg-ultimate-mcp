- Exact 100 cards; Mirko remains commander and the list remains Commander-legal/resolved in the replay output.
- The repair has a direct positive effect: `Massacre Wurm` survives in the final deck. The immediately preceding `d5c349cb...` replay instead retained `Final-Word Phantom` while cutting `Massacre Wurm`.
- Other high-impact payloads remain present, including `Grave Titan`, `Overseer of the Damned`, `Doom Whisperer`, `Dream Eater`, `Ravenous Chupacabra` and `Shriekmaw`.
- Compared with `d5c349cb...`, the final deck replaces `Final-Word Phantom`, `Not Dead After All` and `Corpse Connoisseur` with `Massacre Wurm`, `The Cruelty of Gix` and `Sword of Once and Future`.
- `Massacre Wurm` is a clear strategy-preservation improvement and `The Cruelty of Gix` is strongly aligned as tutor/reanimation support.
- The third change is not convincing Commander improvement. `Sword of Once and Future` adds another combat-damage Equipment dependency to a Mirko deck that already contains `Sword of Light and Shadow` and `Sword of Fire and Ice`; together with `Padeem`, this remains a disconnected subpackage.
- Losing `Corpse Connoisseur` is particularly questionable because it directly loads a creature into the graveyard and can recur through unearth, matching the deck's actual plan better than another Sword. `Not Dead After All` is lower impact, but at least provides cheap creature protection/recursion utility.
- The deck therefore improves payload preservation without resolving the broader replacement-priority/candidate-ranking problem. It still does not look like a strong Bracket-3 reanimator upgrade built around Mirko's surveil and reanimation incentives.
- Specialist-vs-general-AI verdict remains **general-AI clear win**. A strong general AI would be expected to keep the newly preserved high-impact payload while also preferring more graveyard setup, surveil/discard, cheap interaction and direct reanimation support over the repeated Sword/Padeem package.
