## Overall verdict

**REJECT as a new accepted Commander product baseline, but retain the semantic requested-component repair as a meaningful partial generic improvement.**

Keep `e17b0a1cba659b229fd6f0b6e2df79c5e464a616` as the latest accepted fully validated Commander baseline.

The `66836fef...` lineage materially improves symmetry between incoming and outgoing explicit requested-component recognition. The clearest generalization is Elven Empire: Timberwatch Elf, which the rejected `4a7bbf6...` lineage cut despite the explicit `Elf typal + Tokens + Combat / attacks` request, now remains in the final deck. The revised Lathril package is substantially more Elf-centric and removes several of the previous generic drift choices.

However, the five-deck whole-deck review shows that exact theme-clause membership is not sufficient to solve replacement coherence across the benchmark family. Hakbal remains a clear specialist failure, and Bello still loses a defining commander-synergy permanent because the system sees broad `artifact` component membership but does not understand the more specific commander relationship between permanent type, mana value and Bello's animation rule. Ellivere improves component symmetry but still accepts replacements that satisfy broad `enchantment` identity while weakening the Aura-specific execution of the deck.

The remaining repeated weakness is therefore narrower and better localized than before:

**Commander-specific relationship/shape reasoning and payoff-level requested-component recognition remain too shallow after broad component membership is known.**

This is not evidence for harder global theme floors or blanket preservation vetoes. It is evidence that replacement identity needs richer generic relational signals: typal payoff relevance, Aura-carrier/Aura-payoff relevance, commander-referenced permanent types and mana-value/card-shape conditions, and mechanism-to-payoff relationships.

## Fixture review

### Quick Draw / Stella Lee — PASS / strong control retained

The final deck is legal, exactly 100 cards and fully resolved. It remains unmistakably Izzet spellslinger and improves curve, early plays, cheap interaction, tutors, draw, interaction and protection. The accepted additions — including Pact of Negation, Remand, Mental Misstep, March of Swirling Mist and Eldritch Immunity — are structurally coherent with Stella's spell-heavy plan.

There are still debatable individual choices such as Foresight / Manipulate Fate in a deck without a dedicated exile package, and the package is interaction-heavy relative to a casual-upgraded Stella list. But this fixture remains a useful contrasting PASS and shows no new regression from the requested-component repair.

### Virtue and Valor / Ellivere — MIXED / broad component recognition improved, Aura specificity still weak

The final deck is legal, exactly 100 cards and fully resolved. The shell remains enchantment-dense and retains key Aura/enchantment identity pieces such as Songbirds' Blessing, Celestial Archon, Daybreak Coronet, Spectral Steel, Bear Umbra, Snake Umbra, Ethereal Armor and the enchantress engines.

The new semantic component layer correctly recognizes outgoing enchantment cards as belonging to the explicit request more often than the rejected lineage. But broad `Enchantments` membership is still too coarse for Ellivere's actual play pattern. The replay still accepts Angelic Destiny -> Intangible Virtue. Intangible Virtue is an enchantment and supports tokens/combat, so the broad component accounting calls the identity preserved, but Angelic Destiny is a direct Aura-based commander/combat payoff and therefore carries more mechanism-specific value in this deck than the model represents.

The final list contains good upgrades — Sylvan Library, Sterling Grove, Static Prison and Flowering of the White Tree are defensible — but the package remains only partially specialist-quality because broad enchantment equivalence can erase Aura-specific function.

Verdict: **MIXED — semantic component recognition helps, but payoff-level Aura identity remains under-modeled.**

### Explorers of the Deep / Hakbal — FAIL / typal recognition improves representation but replacement quality remains poor

The final deck is legal, exactly 100 cards and fully resolved, and the stock Merfolk/counter core remains visible. However, the upgrade package is still not a credible specialist Hakbal package.

The final deck contains a conspicuous generic/off-plan equipment cluster — Sword of Light and Shadow, Sword of War and Peace, Sword of Fire and Ice and Sword of Forge and Frontier — plus cards such as Archmage Ascension, Tezzeret, Artifice Master and Realm Seekers. These additions do not form a coherent Merfolk/explore/+1/+1-counter optimization package and consume slots that a Commander specialist should prioritize for typal density, explore/counter scaling, efficient protection or on-plan interaction.

The replay also still cuts Reflections of Littjara, now for Sphere Grid. The explanation records that Sphere Grid supports counters/combat, but that does not make the swap strategically equivalent: Reflections is a high-value typal copy engine in a Merfolk deck, while Sphere Grid is a broad counters/combat card. This is exactly the distinction between component membership and payoff/relationship importance that remains missing.

Compared with `4a7bbf6...`, the failure mode has shifted: explicit component membership is less invisible, but the system still cannot reliably rank *how important* a particular typal engine is relative to a merely compatible structural replacement.

Verdict: **FAIL — structural/semantic movement without specialist target achievement.**

### Elven Empire / Lathril — MATERIAL IMPROVEMENT / MIXED-to-PASS quality, not enough to rescue the batch

The final deck is legal, exactly 100 cards and fully resolved. This is the clearest evidence that the repair generalizes usefully.

Most importantly, Timberwatch Elf is retained. In the rejected `4a7bbf6...` final deck, Timberwatch was gone and the additions included Sword of Hearth and Home, Bloom Tender, Grist and Sword of Fire and Ice. In the `66836fef...` final deck, Timberwatch survives and several new inclusions are much more aligned with Elf identity: Fyndhorn Elves, Elvish Reclaimer, Elves of Deep Shadow, Formidable Speaker, Deathrite Shaman, Elvish Lyrist and Fauna Shaman. The package still contains questionable generic choices such as Culling the Weak, Dokuchi Silencer and Sword of Wealth and Power, but the deck now reads materially more like a deliberate Lathril/Elf upgrade rather than a generic Golgari structural package.

The stock list also retains its major typal payoffs: Elvish Archdruid, Imperious Perfect, Lys Alana Huntmaster, Shaman of the Pack, Skemfar Shadowsage, Voice of the Woods, Wolverine Riders, Elderfang Venom, Pride of the Perfect and Prowess of the Fair.

Verdict: **MATERIAL IMPROVEMENT; approximately MIXED-to-PASS on specialist quality.** This is positive repair evidence, but one improved typal fixture does not override the Hakbal/Bello failures.

### Animated Army / Bello — FAIL / broad artifact-enchantment identity is not commander-shape understanding

The final deck is legal, exactly 100 cards and fully resolved. There is one clear improvement over `4a7bbf6...`: Berserkers' Onslaught is now retained, showing that explicit enchantment/component recognition can prevent at least some previous identity erosion.

But the decisive commander-specific failure remains. Esika's Chariot is still cut, this time for Ruby Medallion. The replay explanation says Ruby Medallion supports the `Artifacts` component, but Bello's defining rule is not simply “play artifacts.” Bello animates qualifying noncreature artifacts and enchantments of the relevant mana-value shape into attacking creatures. Esika's Chariot is therefore directly aligned with the commander's permanent-shape incentive; Ruby Medallion is a generic cost reducer whose artifact type only superficially satisfies the broad component label.

The final package also adds generic structural/value cards such as Swiftfoot Boots, Negative Zone Portal, Vexing Puzzlebox, Sword of the Animist, Skullclamp, Expedition Map, Wayfarer's Bauble, Despotic Scepter, Sword of Fire and Ice and Walking Ballista. Some are individually useful, but as a package they continue to pull the deck away from maximizing Bello's high-MV noncreature artifact/enchantment animation plan.

Verdict: **FAIL — broad component recognition is insufficient for commander-specific permanent-shape reasoning.**

## Cross-fixture diagnosis

The replay gives a cleaner architecture boundary than the rejected `4a7bbf6...` run.

What improved:
- Explicit requested type/mechanic membership is now available symmetrically for outgoing cards instead of depending primarily on coarse card roles or bounded search membership.
- Lathril demonstrates real whole-deck benefit: a meaningful Elf combat payoff survives and the final package becomes more typal.
- Bello retaining Berserkers' Onslaught suggests broad enchantment-component preservation can now influence real swaps.
- The Changeling boundary is corrected: every-creature-type semantics cannot falsely turn a Changeling into an Artifact, Enchantment, Aura or Equipment.

What remains repeatedly weak:
- **Payoff importance inside a component.** A typal engine such as Reflections of Littjara is not equivalent to any card that happens to support another requested component.
- **Mechanism specificity.** Ellivere's Aura execution is more specific than broad Enchantment membership.
- **Commander-conditioned card shape.** Bello cares about a relationship between card type, noncreature status and mana-value threshold; broad Artifact/Enchantment labels cannot capture that.
- **Replacement coherence across the whole package.** Hakbal still accumulates individually useful but strategically incoherent generic cards/Swords despite improved component labels.

The next repair threshold is therefore met: the same relational/importance weakness appears across at least Ellivere, Hakbal and Bello, while Lathril provides a positive control showing the preceding semantic membership repair is useful and should not be discarded.

## Next justified product action

Implement the smallest generic **Commander relationship / mechanism-affinity layer** feeding replacement identity, without commander-name or benchmark-specific hardcoding.

The capability should derive relational signals from commander/request/card rules text such as:

1. requested typal membership **and typal payoff/engine relevance**, distinguishing a tribe member/payoff/copy engine from a merely compatible generic card;
2. Aura/enchantment specialization where the commander or requested plan explicitly rewards Auras, modified/enchanted creatures or enchantment attachment rather than treating every enchantment as equivalent;
3. commander-referenced permanent/card-shape conditions — e.g. artifact/enchantment, creature/noncreature, mana-value thresholds, attacking/modified/tapped conditions — and whether a candidate card actually satisfies the condition;
4. payoff/bridge relationships between requested components, so a card that links typal + combat or artifact + commander animation receives stronger identity weight than a card matching one broad label incidentally;
5. advisory relative replacement scoring first; do not make every relational match uncuttable and do not weaken authoritative structural gates when no credible on-plan replacement exists;
6. focused generic regression fixtures spanning typal payoff, Aura specialization, permanent-type/MV shape and a control where a weak theme card still must be cut;
7. full immutable validation, then a **new representative replay**. Do not simply repeat this identical five-precon batch after a wording-only change; include at least one fresh contrasting fixture when validating the next relational repair.

After this defect is either resolved or bounded, shift BENCH-01 breadth toward fresh combo, hybrid, control, aristocrats, budget and unusual-commander cases, with strong general-AI comparison where practical.
